# Astraeus 0.5.0

## astraeus 0.5.0 (2026-10-31)


First download of the packed example (`npm run measure:example`): 1,608,131 bytes raw, 457,845 bytes gzip (code and styles loaded on first open).

A historical probe journey can be told end to end (O-047, G-014), from findings sent back by an outside interplanetary probe app built on 0.4.0.

- Far-away markers and group dots are drawn round instead of square (one shared `dotMaterial`; size and colour unchanged).
- A path reaching past the planets can be zoomed out to whole (O-047 T-203, I-114). In a Sun-centred scene with a path about the Sun and no `camera.setReach` or `camera.fitOrbit`, the farthest zoom-out and the far plane now widen to cover twice the path's farthest distance from the Sun for the canvas shape (it never narrows the old default, and a declared reach still wins); proved with a path to 170 AU on a desktop and a phone shape. Before, the default stopped near the planets and the far plane (5,000 Earth radii) cut the path off.
- Fixed: a moon's orbit ring is centred on the clock, so at the last instant of the timeline it asked for positions the page had not loaded (a chunk error every frame). The loader now also fetches the year after the timeline's end for each moon where the data package ships it.
- Changed: playback in a scene with a timeline bar that does not loop now stops on the end (or start, at a negative speed) of the timeline instead of running past it. Before, the clock kept going and moons were asked for positions the page had not loaded, an error every frame.
- A path can carry different provenance along its length (O-047 T-198, I-113). `addTrajectory` takes `provenanceSpans`: a sorted, non-overlapping `{ start, end, provenance }` list inside the path (the shape of an activity's `provenance`), and the info card shows the span the clock is in; a gap, or no list, shows `provenance` (or the illustrative default) as before. Faults throw `bad-trajectory` naming the index.
- Playback can slow down for encounters and speed up for cruise (O-047 T-201, I-112). `setTimeline` takes `pacing`: spans `{ from, to, rate }` and event rules `{ near: { type, within }, rate }`, with optional `ramp` seconds; the speed eases between pieces (log-space smoothstep, a pure function of the time, so loops and seeks land right). The speed menu gains **Auto** in a scene with pacing; a speed a visitor or the host picks wins until Auto or `setRate("auto")` (new on the `mount` handle and the `AstraeusScene` `rate` prop; `bad-timeline` without pacing). Where pieces meet (a span and an event rule, or two event rules, either containing the other) the slowest speed applies, whatever order they are listed in. Overlapping spans and a rule whose event type has no events throw `bad-timeline` naming the field. New exported type `PacingInput`.
- A host page can read and set the viewer's time and speed (O-047 T-197, I-099). The `mount` handle gains `time`, `rate`, `setTime(time)`, `setRate(rate)` and `onChange(listener)`, and `mount` takes `onChange`; `AstraeusScene` gains `time`, `rate` and `onChange` props. `onChange` hears `{ time, rate, playing, cause }` for playback, a visitor's use of the timeline and the host's own calls. `setTime` outside the timeline throws `bad-time`; `setRate` takes only a speed the timeline offers, else `bad-timeline`; setting before `ready` throws `missing-data`. New exported types `ViewerChange` and `RateSource`.
- Changed: the speed menu shows exactly the `rates` a scene gives (I-106). Before, 1x, 1 hour, 1 day and 1 week per second were always added. A scene that gives no `rates` still offers 1 minute, 1 hour and 1 day per second. A scene that relied on the added speeds must now list them.
- Orbit lines and `camera.fitOrbit` now work for every planet (O-047 T-196, I-111). One rule, chosen by data coverage and never by name: one full turn of the body's own positions where they cover a whole turn from the opening time (the turn search now reaches Saturn's 29.5 years), else the two-body ellipse osculating at the opening time (Uranus, Neptune), whose info card says so and that perturbations are not drawn. The farthest distance for `fitOrbit` is worked out once in setup (at `toDefinition`, or at `prepare` once the planet series are in), so a failure reaches the host once through `ready`, not per frame.
- A body's positions can cover its own years (O-047 T-195, I-110 engine half). A moon-fit `index.json` may give each body a `span: { fromUtc, toUtc }` (optional; an index without one reads as before, the six shipped data packages are unchanged), and the engine answers each body only inside its own span and its centre's. The importer `tools/import/horizons-moons.mjs --only ... --from ... --to ...` merges a longer-span rebuild of chosen bodies into an existing index.
- New `SceneError` code `out-of-span`. A scene whose timeline reaches outside the years a loaded moon's data (or the built-in planet series, 1950 to 2045) covers now fails once, as `ready` rejecting at mount, naming the body, the data package and the span, instead of throwing on every frame.
- The main moons and each planet's system fitted back to 1950 (O-047 T-200, I-110 data half). `astraeus-data-jupiter` (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), `-saturn` (Mimas, Enceladus, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Titan, Hyperion, Iapetus, Phoebe), `-uranus` (Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon) and `-neptune` (Triton, Nereid) now answer from 1950-01-01, each with its planet; every other moon keeps 2010 to 2030. Worst fit error 0.59 m or less per moon, 0.05 m or less per planet; moon about planet through the provider against Horizons 0.53 m or less. The packages grew: Jupiter 22.9 to 30.9 MB packed, Saturn 40.0 to 53.5 MB, Uranus 32.6 to 40.6 MB, Neptune 17.8 to 18.5 MB (a page still downloads only the years it shows). The built-in planet series (`planets.json`) now runs 1950 to 2045 (was 1969), 329 kB to 432 kB raw (220 to 279 kB gzip); the example's first download is 1,608,131 bytes raw, 457,845 gzip (was 1,594,919 and 453,695). A mapped moon's `accuracy.validUtc` and its position note follow the span of its own data.
- Fixed: the fitted moons' time-scale table before 1972 followed monthly samples, so a Jupiter- or Saturn-centred position in 1961 to 1972 could be 1 to 7 km off in time terms; it is now sampled daily with a piece per step and per straight stretch (within 10 microseconds), and the end-to-end check back to 1950 is within 0.6 m.
- Fixed: asking the ephemeris for a body's bounds threw for Pluto's data, whose last chunk ends a day past its time-scale table.

## astraeus-data 0.5.0 (2026-10-31)


No changes to the pictures or this package's code. Released with `astraeus` 0.5.0 to keep the version numbers together. The moon-position packages changed: `astraeus-data-jupiter`, `-saturn`, `-uranus` and `-neptune` now hold their main moons and planet from 1950-01-01 (see the `astraeus` 0.5.0 changelog); `-mars` and `-pluto` are unchanged.
