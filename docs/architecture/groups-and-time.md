#### Groups and the `points` layer (O-009)

A group is many objects with one scientific State source. `PointGroup` (`src/core/pointGroup.ts`) declares count, centre (Earth or Sun are composed), EQJ frame, optional time bounds and provenance, fills every member's EQJ km position into a caller-owned buffer for a UTC time, and returns one member as an ordinary `State` (body id `<group id>:<index>`). `createOrbitGroup` is the generic orbit-based group: base elements in a named plane (`ecliptic-j2000` or `EQJ`), a uniform half-width spread per element, a seed (same seed and time give bit-identical positions), two-body propagation through `TwoBodyAdapter`, and non-bound members rejected. `State`, `Trajectory`, `Provenance`, `TimelineEvent` and `ScalePolicy` are unchanged.

An experience opts in with the `points` layer and `groups: GroupLayer[]` on its definition; `groupsOf` and `experienceWithGroups` give one deduplicated shell item per group, carrying the group's provenance, bounds and limitations. `PointsView` (`src/app/PointsView.ts`) holds two exact fills per group, one at or before the clock time and one at or after it, `refillAfterMs` apart (default 5 minutes), and draws a straight-line blend of them each frame so dots glide; playing on slides the pair and fills one new end per span, a seek fills once (presentation only, State untouched; at a fill time the drawn positions are exact; the blend is off by about 8 km at worst on a 26,000 km ring at the default span, measured in T-030). It composes Sun-centred members to the scene's Earth-centred placement through existing centre composition, then applies `ScalePolicy`, render axes and the floating origin. A group is hidden outside its bounds with no fill. Its focus, follow and label point is the centroid; its id is a `FocusId`. Camera presets for a group come from the experience.

O-017 changed how `PointsView` draws, without changing State or the `PointGroup` contract's meaning (T-073, T-075). (1) Look-ahead slicing: `PointGroup.fillPositionsRangeKm(t, out, start, end)` is an optional range fill (same values as the whole fill); a group with it and more than 1,024 members computes its next fill in chunks within `GroupLayer.refillSliceMs` per frame (default 8, 0 turns it off; shower files may set `look.refillSliceMs`), and swaps it in only when complete. It falls back to a whole fill on overrun, a seek, a change of direction or a scale-policy change. (2) GPU blend: the two fills are uploaded as `position` and `positionTo`, relative to an anchor kept in 64 bits, and the vertex shader blends them with `uBlend` and applies the floating-origin shift as `uOffset`, so frames that only move the blend upload nothing; attributes are rewritten only when a fill changes. (3) Kepler on the graphics chip: `src/core/keplerElements.ts` gives each member its elements (`PointGroup.keplerElements?()`, provided by `createOrbitGroup` and `createListedOrbitGroup`), `src/app/keplerShader.ts` holds the GLSL and its 32-bit JavaScript twin (the tests compare it with the 64-bit group positions), and `src/app/KeplerPoints.ts` draws the points with the vertex shader solving Kepler's equation (start guess, then Halley steps: 2, 3 or 4 by eccentricity). Time is a uniform relative to an epoch chosen in 64 bits on the CPU; the epoch is re-based when the clock is more than a window away (0.5 rad of the fastest mean motion). Fallback rule: a group with no `keplerElements`, 1,024 members or fewer, or a `ScalePolicy` without `linearUnitsPerKm` (optional; set on True, Readable and Solar-system) keeps path (2). In shader mode fills serve only the centroid and extent (focus, label, framing): exact fills 7 days apart (`KEPLER_CENTROID_SPAN_MS`), interpolated between. The shader draws the true position where the straight-line blend cut the corner of tight perihelion arcs (43 km against 3.5 million km worst case in Geminids, `tests/pointsBlendCorner.test.ts`); the four shower pictures moved by that on the arcs near the Sun, and the owner accepted them as the new picture baseline. Not measured: real GPU `sin`/`cos` accuracy and real-phone frame rates (owner's phone look is owed).

**Frame-rate counter (T-031).** `FrameStats` (`src/app/frameStats.ts`) is a pure fixed-window (120 frames, no allocation per frame) collector of frame durations; it reports frames per second over the last second, the longest recent frame and frames over 33.4 ms, and restarts after a gap over 1 s (a paused tab). `App` creates it only while Developer mode is on and hands it to the scene through `SceneControls.frameStats` (null means nothing is measured); the scene ticks it once per frame and copies each `PointsView.fillCount` into `SceneControls.groupFills`. The existing 250 ms poll reads both into `FrameRateSection` (`src/app/FrameRateSection.tsx`), a Developer tools section that carries a note that the figures are for this browser and machine. Presentation only: no State, scale or camera involvement, no new metric kind, no `src/core/` or `src/shell/` change.

#### Time windows and rates (O-010)

A window is a labelled period that lasts, independent of any trajectory. `TimelineWindow` (`src/core/timeWindow.ts`) has an id, label, start, end, an optional peak inside it, optional rate samples with a unit label, and optional provenance spans inside it. `createWindows` validates and freezes them with named errors (start before end, peak and samples inside, sorted unique sample times, finite non-negative values, sorted non-overlapping spans, unique ids). `rateAt` is linear between samples and null outside the window, outside the samples or without a rate. `provenanceAt` returns the span covering a time (a shared boundary belongs to the later span) or null; a window without spans falls back to the experience's `eventProvenance`. `TimelineEvent`, `State`, `Trajectory`, `Provenance` and `ScalePolicy` are unchanged, and nothing in a window is derived from a State (ARCH-02).

`ExperienceConfig.windows` is optional. An experience without it (Apollo) renders and navigates exactly as before. The shell draws each window on the timeline bar as a shaded band, a faint rate curve and a peak marker (`timelineModel` builds positions and the curve path). The context row adds an "Inside" line with the current rate while the clock is in a window. `navigationStops` merges event times with each window's start, peak and end for Previous/Next; where an event and a window point share a time the event is the stop. Opening a window uses a `window` info target and `infoView(..., timeUtcMs)`, so its rate and source depend on the clock and the source wording changes across a span boundary. A new shell metric kind `rate` ("N per unit time") reuses value, unit, range and context and renders through the existing Metric Visuals; an experience supplies it through `createMetrics` from the same window samples (`rateAt`), so the curve and the readout share one source.

Made-up evidence (T-035): `src/synthetic/syntheticDefinition.ts` declares a "Contact window" with a rising and falling "passes per hour" rate, a peak, and two `illustrative` provenance spans whose accuracy and notes differ. Its object's metrics add a contact-rate readout (unavailable outside the window). No generic code was needed to mount it. One generic fix was needed: the window layer sat below the seek slider, so the band and peak could not be clicked; its stacking was raised to match the event markers.
