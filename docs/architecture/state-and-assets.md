## Interfaces and Data Flow

`SimulationClock` produces integer UTC Unix milliseconds from a monotonic anchor. `MoonTrajectory.stateAt(time, center)` returns Moon State in EQJ for Earth or Sun centers. Earth orientation comes from the provider adapter; the Moon near-side axis and local pole basis come from the dated trajectory. `SampledTrajectory.stateAt(time)` returns Earth-centred EQJ km for a vehicle, cubic Hermite when velocity is present, and throws `TrajectoryOutOfRangeError` outside its `bounds`; the scene draws a tracked body only inside its bounds. `CenteredPosition` composition changes the origin while preserving body identity. `ScalePolicy` maps physical kilometres/radii without changing State. Sun geometry is represented by an explicit Sun/Earth position and derived physical directions/illuminated fraction. The public core has no Orb, React or Three.js types.

The app adapter builds `MissionConfig` from the generated Apollo samples and events. Each `TrackedBody` carries its trajectory, sample/anchor times, optional anchor labels, `positionDiscrepancies` and the trajectory's `Provenance`; `TrackedReadout.positionKm` is the current physical position alongside frame, centre, range and speed. `MissionConfig.journey` supplies the configured journey endpoints. `TrackedBodyView` draws the source anchors with a generated round sprite (`anchorSpriteTexture`) instead of untextured square points; the positions and count are unchanged.

`buildApollo11Experience` projects app/mission data into the generic shell contract. `ExperienceObject` carries configured `description`, `provenance`, `knownLimitations`, label priority and distance fields; the experience carries `cameraPresets`, events, time bounds, rates and `eventProvenance`. Generated trajectory provenance passes through `TrackedBody.provenance`; `src/app/ephemerisProvenance.ts` re-exports the Earth/Moon ephemeris records, which now live in `src/core/bodies/`. Metric values are calculated by app adapters from physical State and passed to the shell through `metricsFor`.

At startup `App` selects `true-scale`, and `SceneControls.policy` is `trueScale`. It passes `ExperienceConfig`, `ClockSnapshot`, `scaleId`, `metricsFor`, scene children and callbacks into `AstraeusShell`. The shell owns selection, active camera preset and open information-panel state. Selection and camera actions call back to app-owned refs; play, rate and seek callbacks operate the `SimulationClock`; the scale callback maps `true-scale`/`readable-scale` through `scalePolicyFor` to the existing `trueScale`/`readableScale` policies. Neither the shell nor a presentation change mutates scientific State. Developer diagnostics remain app-owned and are mounted only when the developer toggle is on.

### Experience plug-in boundary

`App` takes one `ExperienceDefinition` (`src/app/experienceDefinition.ts`) and never imports an experience. The definition holds the shell's `ExperienceConfig`, the clock start and rate, the `sceneLayers` it opts into (`tracked-bodies`, `points`, `sun-centred`; the `earth-moon` layer was retired in O-014, composition supplies the bodies), its `TrackedBody` list, a `createMetrics` factory and optional developer-panel sections. Tracked bodies are drawn only when that layer is listed. `src/main.tsx` is the only place that picks an experience. The scene mounts the bodies of the experience's composition; with no composition it mounts no bodies, lights or Moon path, makes no Moon or Sun calls, and frames the camera on the declared `sceneCenter` (the canvas carries `data-bodies`, empty then); `synthetic-bare` in the entry exercises this (I-015). Apollo (`src/experiences/apollo11/apollo11Definition.tsx`) and the synthetic fixture (`src/synthetic/`) both mount through it; neither `src/core/` nor `src/shell/` changed.

### Asset architecture (O-014)

**Catalogue and resolver.** A `CelestialBodyDefinition` (`src/core/body.ts`) holds a body's id, parent, radii, GM, orientation rule and `accuracy` (position record with `validUtc`); built-ins live in `src/core/bodies/` and carry their ephemeris records (the former `src/app/ephemerisProvenance.ts` is a re-export, ARCH-04). A `StateSourceBinding` gives each body its `Trajectory`. `createCatalogue(definitions, bindings, visuals)` takes three lists, so a visual can exist without a definition and be rejected. `StateResolver.stateOf(body, t, centre)` composes through the parent chain for any pair of catalogued ids; `sunFrom(centre, t)` returns the catalogue root as a `State`; `offsetKm` gives centre-to-centre offsets. Failures are `CatalogueError` (a `RangeError`). Composed States carry position only: no catalogued provider supplies velocity, and a provider that did would need velocity composition added to the resolver. `createState(input, requiresOrientation?)` takes the orientation rule from the resolver's definitions. `createOrbTwoBodyAdapter(definitions)` and `TwoBodyTrajectoryInput.center` carry a non-Sun GM to the two-body maths; `TwoBodyAdapter`, `OrbitGroupInput`, `State`, `Trajectory` and `Provenance` are unchanged. The resolver's memo holds 64 entries (FIFO).

**Visual records and one renderer.** `BodyVisual` records (`src/assets/visual.ts`: sphere or spheroid, texture reference with licence, atmosphere, material, light, marker, yaw) live per body in `src/assets/bodies/`. `BodyView` (`src/app/BodyView.tsx`, the React mount) draws from pure decisions in `src/app/bodyDraw.ts`: sphere with or without texture, atmosphere, marker (the Sun) and spheroid (Jupiter) with independently mapped equatorial and polar radii; `drawnRadii` and the focus limits pass the body id to `mapRadius`. `SunBody` is reduced to the Sun's shell item and validation; colour and marker size come from the catalogue Sun, and `visualOverrides` (`visible`, `exaggeration`, any appearance field merged over the record) is the experience's choice. A body with `visible: false` is not mounted but still resolves as a centre. `bodyMetrics.ts` and `catalogueItem.ts` sit in `src/app/`, not `src/assets/`, because they need shell types and `tests/layerRule.test.ts` lets `src/assets/` import only core, assets and three (ARCH-04).

**Compositions and scene centre.** `SystemComposition` (`src/assets/compositions.ts`) names catalogue ids, a default centre and declared `CompositionPath`s (`{body, spanMs, samples}`); it names no experience. `ExperienceDefinition.composition` is optional only for the bodiless fixture `synthetic-bare` (which then must declare `sceneCenter`); every shipped experience names one. `sceneCenterOf` defaults to the composition's centre and rejects a centre outside the composition. The scene (`AstraeusScene`, formerly `EarthMoonScene`) mounts one `BodyView` per composition body; `SceneRuntime` carries `resolver`, `bodies`, `composition`, `sceneCenter`; `ExperienceRuntime` is `{clock, resolver, catalogue}` (the separate `MoonTrajectory` and `adapter` are gone; the Apollo panels read the Moon through the resolver). The old `earth-moon` scene layer is retired; the canvas carries `data-scene-center`, `data-bodies` and `data-composition-paths`. Framing and layer assignment are in `src/app/sceneFraming.ts`: the centre body gets a minimum of 1.15 radii and a default maximum of max(3.2, 2.4 x span); other spheres 1.6 and 4 radii; markers are framed by their paths' reach. Camera reach is per experience (`ExperienceDefinition.camera`, O-011), which closes the camera-limits gap below. Moon-focus start side is generalised to "toward the body's parent".

**Tracked paths in Sun-centred scenes (O-046).** `addTrajectory` is accepted in a Sun-centred scene. A tracked path is one relative trail: each sample is the position from `lineFixedTo` (any scene body; default the scene centre) at the sample's own time (`relativeTrailKm(samples, centre, placement, against?)`), then the line is carried to that body's place from the scene centre at the clock time, so `lineFixedTo: "sun"` holds a probe's line still against the Sun. Path input is EQJ about its centre (`center`, default the scene's first body, owner decision 2026-10-30); `plane: "ecliptic-j2000"` rotates positions and velocities once at entry with the orb adapter's matrix, while a provider state's `orientation` passes through unrotated (known limit). Label reach is one rule for bodies and paths (none in a Sun-centred or fitted scene, else the camera reach, at least 500) with a per-item `labelReach` override. Tracked paths report to `data-paths` for the browser check.

**Path kinds.** `PathLayer` is `PathRule & PathSpec & {object}`; a rule is an orbit (a path about a parent, co-moving) or a trail (relative, inertial), and `span` is `TimeBounds` or a `RollingSpan {windowMs}` about the clock time (the Moon's monthly orbit). Rules live in `src/app/pathRules.ts`; `PathStyle` (opacity, depth test, layer, render order) is presentation and never reaches a rule. Path lines draw after data dots (render order 1 against 0), so a path stays visible across a belt (I-020). The resolver supplies the root frame through `sunFrom`, so no literal "sun" is needed in generic code. Earth's year in Perseids is declared by the experience (fixed one-year span about the peak, 361 samples); the parent comet's orbit is drawn for peak plus or minus 180 days only (the full 109P orbit reaches about 51 AU, beyond the declared camera reach) and is `illustrative`, because its place along the orbit is set by the stream's node choice. The planets' orbit paths come from the catalogue (every body whose parent is the root and not in the composition), so a catalogued planet adds its orbit to every Sun-centred shower.

**Scale policies.** `ScaleId` is a plain string. `SolarSystemScalePolicy` keeps distances true and multiplies radii by a declared per-body factor (`factorFor(bodyId?)`, `explanation`); `scalePolicyFor(id, supplied?)` accepts application-supplied policies and `AstraeusShell` takes an optional `scaleChoices` prop that passes to `ScaleControl`. `App` builds the solar-system policy from the experience's `exaggeration` overrides (default factor 1). The public `solarSystemScale` (O-046) is that policy's shared id: `setScale(solarSystemScale)` opens a Sun-centred scene with an enlarged body on it, and outside such a scene `toDefinition` throws `bad-scale`. Jupiter-Io offers True scale only: Readable (distances x0.1) would bury Io inside Jupiter (orbit 5.9 Jupiter radii drawn at 0.59), and the solar-system scale enlarges both bodies.

**Planets.** `AstronomyAdapter` gained `planetPositionFromSunKm(planet, t)` and `daysSinceJ2000Tt(t)` (the IAU models count TT days; only the adapter imports Orb). Until O-025 the adapter registered the full VSOP87A series (the compact default is off by 2,877 to 142,044 km), costing about 1.4 MB raw (0.56 MB gzip) of bundle: at O-014 the main bundle went from 1,537.6 kB (458.8 kB gzip) to 2,938.7 kB (1,022.5 kB); 2,947.5 kB (1,025.1 kB) at T-050. I-022 (`1eb8b05`) moved the series out: `src/core/planetSeries.ts` exposes `loadPlanetSeries()` (one cached dynamic import of `planetModels.ts`), `planetModels()` (throws before loading rather than falling back to the compact tables) and `isPlanetId`; `src/app/planetNeeds.ts` (`needsPlanetSeries`) tells `src/main.tsx` whether the chosen definition mounts or is centred on a planet, and only then does `start()` await the load before rendering. Each experience is itself a dynamic import in `main.tsx`. Main chunk 3,041.8 to 1,185.1 kB (`docs/evidence/i022-20261016T115528Z/`); Apollo and Earth-Moon never download the planet chunk. Rotation data sit beside each definition (`MARS_ROTATION` etc., with a citation) and `planetTrajectory.ts` is the one generic planet source. Position maxima against JPL Horizons over six dates (1969-07-21 to 2030-06-01, which is each planet's `validUtc`): Mercury 30.4 km, Venus 50.1 km, Mars 95.8 km, Jupiter 1,269.4 km (0.325 arcsec). Planets are in the catalogue (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter); none is mounted by Apollo or Earth-Moon. **Planets as curve fits (O-025 T-101).** The Orb series is no longer registered: `planetModels.ts` builds each planet's model from Chebyshev fits of VSOP87A stored in `data/ephemerides/planets/planets.json` (heliocentric ecliptic J2000, TT, km), span 1969 to 2045; a date outside it throws a `RangeError` rather than extrapolating. The same lazy path serves it (`loadPlanetSeries`, `needsPlanetSeries`), the planet chunk shrank, and the position maxima above stand as the VSOP87A reference the fits are checked against (`docs/evidence/o025-t101-20261019/planet-fits.json`, `planets-size.json`). O-033 (T-114, T-116) extended the planet fits and bodies: Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are VSOP87A fits like the first four; Pluto is a fit of the Pluto system barycentre from JPL Horizons (about 11 KB, `planets.json` 511 to 329 KB), with Pluto's own offset from the barycentre added once `pluto-moons` is loaded (`PLUTO_BINDING`), so every other planet view stops paying for Pluto's wobble and a Pluto view loads `pluto-moons` first (`prepare`). The catalogue now holds the Sun and Mercury to Pluto. The showers keep the outer planets out of their views (`BEYOND_SHOWER_VIEWS` in `showerBasics.ts`, used by `showerBodies.ts` and `showerPaths.ts`) so the Perseids and Geminids views are unchanged; whether the owner wants the outer planets in the shower overview is an open owner question, not an architecture change.

**Jupiter and Io.** `jupiterIoSystem` mounts about Jupiter, a centre that is neither Earth nor the Sun, through the same binding, resolver, composition, scene and shell as the Moon, with no change to `StateSourceBinding`, `CelestialBodyDefinition`, `createTwoBodyTrajectory`, `ScalePolicy`, `SystemComposition` or `ExperienceDefinition`. Until O-025 Io was published JPL mean elements about Jupiter's GM through the two-body provider, wrapped with tidal-lock orientation inside `core/bodies/io.ts`; since T-098 Io and the other mapped moons are placed from the core Horizons curve fits (`jupiterMoons.ts`, Jupiter-centred, valid 2010-2030 only), and the mean-element error figures in this sentence describe the earlier provider (the 9,387 km figure and the `approximate demonstration` status no longer describe Io's shown position, which the fits reproduce to within a few centimetres of Horizons; the per-body figures are in the T-096 evidence). The demo now opens at 2016-11-04 for two weeks (outside the fits the old 2000 window would throw; a year-long window and a Juno story were planned as O-032, removed by the owner on 2026-10-21: Juno is to be a separate project), the page fetches only the 2016 chunks plus history chunks 2014-2015 (`import.meta.glob`, shipped and loaded by `prepare`) (a generic tidally-locked wrapper is a candidate to lift when a third moon arrives). Measured against Horizons at six dates the maximum Jupiter-centred error is 9,387.47 km, 2.58 Io diameters (3,642.98 km): outside the one-diameter line, so Io is an `approximate demonstration` (`reconstructed`, figure and the source's own warning in `knownLimitations`); at J2000 it is 825.5 km. The retrograde apsidal-motion sign is not stated by the source and was chosen to reproduce Io's period. Jupiter carries a Solar System Scope 2k map, CC BY 4.0, based on NASA imagery (I-024, `src/assets/bodies/jupiter.ts`, page opened 2026-10-16); its yaw is a visual stand-in because the bands rotate differentially, and the donor colour is the fallback under it. Io uses the donor's USGS public-domain map. Since O-033 this is the Jupiter view of the Planets demo (`?experience=planets`, Jupiter the default; `?experience=jupiter-io` opens it still): its window, rates and text moved to `data/planets/jupiter.json`, and `systems/jupiter.ts` names its data. Io (and the other synchronous moons) now face their planet by the IAU model through the generic loader rather than the tidal-lock wrapper's own constants (Io within 0.8 degrees of the old facing, test tolerance 1 degree); its shown position comes from the fits, as since T-098.

**Lighting choice (Decision 4).** Each lit body keeps its own directional light along its own physical Sun direction, on its own render layer, in a pass loop over the composition (pure `bodyLightDirections` in `src/app/lighting.ts`; the cap is the render layers, 29 to 31 lit bodies depending on the Task note, not measured). A single point light at the Sun's scaled position was measured against it (a measurement mode, `?lighting=single-sun`; the mode and its switch were removed in O-017 T-068, per-body lights are the only lighting): illuminated fraction differs by at most 7.2e-9 and direction by 1.5e-6 degrees on the four USNO phase events at True and Readable scales; rendered Earth, Jupiter and Io frames differ by at most 0.006 of 255 on average; Moon frames differ by 7.7 to 13.0 of 255 because one light has one strength (Earth is tuned 1.8/0.18, the Moon 1.4/0.08), and by 0.0005 to 0.009 once given the Moon's strength. A single directional light at the centre's direction would be 0.16 degrees off at the Moon. Decision: keep per-body lights for Spike 04; the single point light is viable for larger systems, at the cost of moving per-body intensity into material or per-body exposure. `illumination.ts` computes `illuminatedFraction` by role (any satellite, the Sun as source); `lunarIlluminatedFraction` is a wrapper with its Moon guard.

**Texture rights.** Every texture is data on its visual record (`licence.ts`): either a recorded licence or `localOnly`, never both. Since I-029 (`a2f82e0`) no texture is `localOnly`: Earth is NASA Blue Marble Next Generation (July 2004, public domain) and the Moon is the NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit LROC WAC map (SVS 4720, public domain); Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter are Solar System Scope CC BY 4.0 (the Jupiter page was opened 2026-10-16; Mercury, Venus and Mars rest on the donor README); Io is USGS public domain per the donor README. The mechanism stays: a public build (`ASTRAEUS_PUBLIC_BUILD=1`) replaces each module named in the local-only list in `vite.config.ts` by `src/assets/textures/public-placeholder.png` at the import (the list is now empty), and `tools/validate/public-build-assets.mjs` hashes `dist/` against the local-only sources, `data/local/` and the Yale file. The donor's remaining planet and moon pictures are traced but not shipped (I-032, open).

**Name rule.** `tests/nameCheck.test.ts` forbids experience names everywhere in `src/core/`, `src/assets/`, `src/shell/` and generic `src/app/`, and body names (`"sun"`, `"earth"`, `"moon"`, `EARTH`, `MOON`, `SUN`) everywhere except `src/core/bodies/` and `src/assets/bodies/` and ten allowlisted files, each with a stated reason: `src/assets/compositions.ts`, `src/core/{astronomyAdapter,body,illumination,moonTrajectory,referenceCenters,scalePolicy,state,sunCentredSources,twoBodyAdapter}.ts`. This differs from design section E check 7 ("`src/assets/` may name bodies; no body name in `src/core/`"): the core providers are the Orb boundary and the root of the body chain, so the allowlist stands, and a stale entry fails the test. Section E check 7 is amended to say so; moving the providers under `src/core/bodies/` is not planned.

**Precision at 1 AU (T-045).** A true-scale Earth 1 AU from the Sun marker, projected in Float64 from the Float32 buffers the GPU receives for 60 frames (measured by `browser-precision.mjs` in the headless software renderer; since O-038 T-144 the same bound, one pixel, is the fast test `tests/sceneTransforms.test.ts` "position precision far from the Sun"): static displacement 1e-9 px (Earth) and 3e-8 px (path vertex); camera-sweep residual 0.00058 px (Earth) and 0.00056 px (path), 0.0013 px zoomed out. The Float32 grid at 1 AU is 12.4 km (5.4e-6 px at the Sun view). The focused body never exceeds 1 px (floating origin, threshold unchanged). The figures exclude the GPU's own Float32 matrix arithmetic. The T-050 re-run reproduces them (sun-focus sweep 0.00058 px Earth, 0.00056 px path).

**Moon `validUtc`.** The Moon's `accuracy.validUtc` is the sampled range, 2022-06-01 to 2025-06-01, not a model validity; Apollo (1969) lies outside it. Planets and Io use their sampled ranges likewise (planets 1969-07-21 to 2030-06-01 as measured; the planet fits are valid 1969 to 2045, and Io and the mapped moons 2010 to 2030); Earth 1900 to 2100. Only the shower builder reads `validUtc` today (to state where Jupiter's orbit is extrapolated past 2030). The Earth-Moon Explorer's window is 2025-01-01 to 2025-04-01, inside the Moon's checked range, not the design sketch's 2026. O-033: the new moons use the same span as Io and the mapped Jupiter moons, 2010-01-01 to 2030-01-01 (`MOON_FIT_SPAN` in `moonSystem.ts`, one span for every system), Saturn to Pluto sit on the planet fits (valid 1969 to 2045, Pluto's barycentre likewise), and the Planets demo opens each planet on 2016-11-04 for two weeks, inside every span; no code outside the shower builder reads `validUtc`.
