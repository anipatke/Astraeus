# Apollo 11 Reconstruction — Validation Report

> Generated by `npm run apollo11:reconstruct` from `tools/apollo11`. Do not edit by hand; edit the tool or its config and re-run.

This is a **reconstruction** of the Apollo 11 flight path from sparse NASA postflight anchor states (Mission Report MSC-00171 / SP-238). It is not the exact flight path and makes no accuracy claim between anchors beyond the measurements below.

## Method by segment

| Segment kind | Method |
|---|---|
| Parking orbit (Earth orbit insertion → TLI ignition) | No NASA anchor exists before TLI ignition (A-01). A-01 integrated **backward** in time with the coast dynamics below. Unanchored at the start; drag is ignored. |
| Coast between anchors (including Earth ↔ Moon) | Numerical integration (RK4, fixed step) in Earth-centred EQJ with Earth point mass + J2, and the Moon and Sun as third bodies from the Astraeus adapter. Integrated forward from the earlier anchor and backward from the later one, then smoothed (see Joins). |
| Ordinary burn (ignition → cutoff anchors) | Numerical integration from the ignition state with Earth (J2), Moon and Sun gravity plus a constant EQJ acceleration solved to reach the cutoff velocity. The cutoff position residual is reported; the next coast starts from the modelled cutoff state. This is not guidance. |
| Powered descent (PDI → touchdown) | Two-anchor cubic Hermite in the Moon-fixed frame. The 2022 NASA descent reconstruction was not used (docs/APOLLO11_SOURCES.md section 3). |
| Surface stay | The touchdown site held fixed in the Moon-fixed frame (IAU lunar rotation model). |
| Powered ascent (lift-off → ascent insertion) | Two-anchor cubic Hermite in the Moon-fixed frame, from the touchdown site at the lift-off event time. |

Moon-relative states are normalised offline to Earth-centred EQJ with the Astraeus Moon state at the same instant. Earth-fixed anchors use the Fischer 1960 ellipsoid and Orb's ECEF→EQJ transform; Moon-fixed anchors use the IAU WGCCRE lunar rotation on a 1,738.09 km sphere for position; Moon-referenced flight-path angle and heading are taken in a local basis whose north points to the EQJ (J2000) pole, adopted because the lunar-orbit anchors are only consistent that way (owner decision 2026-10-06; the source does not define it). Conventions are in `docs/APOLLO11_SOURCES.md` section 4.

**Joins (smoothing).** Propagated forward from its start state alone, a coast still misses its next anchor; that **raw miss** is measured and published below. After a burn, this forward state is the modelled cutoff state; otherwise it is the converted start anchor. The coast is blended to a backward propagation from the next anchor with a smoothstep weight (0 with zero slope at the start, 1 with zero slope at the end), interpolating radius linearly and direction along the great circle about the Moon when both anchors are Moon-referenced, otherwise about Earth. Non-cutoff anchors are met in position and velocity, and `discontinuities` is empty. The largest difference between the smoothed and raw forward path is published per coast as the correction. Large corrections are a presentation repair over doubtful data, not physics.

**Sample interval.** Per segment, the largest interval from a fixed ladder whose cubic Hermite error at held-out midpoints is at most 0.25 km. The measured maximum is reported below.

### A-05 — flightPathAngleDeg

- Printed source value: 44.94
- Reconstruction value: 49.94
- Status: `probable-source-typo`; confidence: high, not documentary-confirmed
- Owner-approved reconstruction exception (T-007, Issue I-002). The raw and canonical source value stays 44.94; this is an inferred override, not a source correction, and applies to no other anchor.
- Evidence:
  - Propagation from the surrounding anchors independently predicts about 49.95 degrees.
  - 49.94 degrees preserves trajectory and angular-momentum continuity with neighbouring anchors.
  - Contemporaneous PAO trajectory readouts after docking (GET 3:46 and 4:04) agree materially better with the 49.94 degree trajectory.
  - 44.94 degrees creates large unexplained residuals into neighbouring anchors.
  - T-014 searched the NASA post-launch mission report, the technical air-to-ground and PAO transcripts, the Apollo Flight Journal docking transcript, and NASA-CR-108349. ## Known gaps and unsupported claims

- No accuracy claim is made between anchors. The raw misses above show how far the propagated path is from the next anchor before smoothing; the smoothed path hides that step on screen but does not make the path more accurate.
- Coasts smoothed by more than 100 km or 50 m/s: A-02>A-03 (columbia, 463 km / 69263 m/s); A-03>A-04 (columbia, 76 km / 77 m/s); A-06>A-07 (columbia, 120 km / 127 m/s); A-08>A-09 (columbia, 116 km / 2 m/s); A-14>A-15 (columbia, 820 km / 721 m/s); A-15>A-16 (columbia, 469 km / 486 m/s); A-17>A-27 (columbia, 1366 km / 1209 m/s); A-27>A-28 (columbia, 264 km / 286 m/s); A-28>A-29 (columbia, 490 km / 1634 m/s); A-30>A-31 (columbia, 1963 km / 1745 m/s); A-32>A-33 (columbia, 627 km / 20 m/s); A-34>A-35 (columbia, 778 km / 68 m/s); A-35>A-36 (columbia, 32 km / 77 m/s); A-15>A-18 (eagle, 456 km / 377 m/s); A-25>A-26 (eagle, 148 km / 134 m/s); A-26>A-27 (eagle, 117 km / 322 m/s). Their anchors disagree with each other under these dynamics. The 10-second A-02>A-03 coast starts from the modelled A-01>A-02 cutoff, whose 454.7 km position residual is listed above. Elsewhere, likely causes include printed headings that do not fit the orbit plane, lunar gravity anomalies omitted by the point-mass Moon, and print rounding. Treat the path there as illustrative.
- Moon-referenced flight-path angle and heading are read against Earth-equatorial (EQJ) north. Table 7-II does not say which north applies at the Moon; the reading is adopted because, under it, the lunar-orbit anchors share one orbit plane and A-10>A-11 and A-32>A-33 nearly close (`docs/ASTRAEUS_SPIKE_02.md` section 7.2). Under lunar north they scatter by up to about 17°.
- A-33/A-34 speeds are printed as 4 375.0 / 4 374.0 ft/s with an illegible hundreds digit; owner-approved inferred overrides use 4 075.0 / 4 074.0 ft/s (see Inferred overrides). This is not documentary-confirmed from the primary scan.
- A-05's flight-path angle is printed as 44.94° but disagrees with its neighbours; the reconstruction uses an owner-approved **inferred override** of 49.94° (probable source typo, high confidence, not documentary-confirmed). The raw value is unchanged and the override is recorded in `data/apollo11/normalised/anchors.json`. Overrides are manual, owner-approved exceptions (A-05, A-33, A-34), not a general data-repair mechanism.
- The parking orbit is propagated backward from the TLI-ignition anchor with no NASA anchor of its own.
- Ordinary burns use constant inertial acceleration with Earth J2, Moon and Sun gravity. Their cutoff velocities are fitted and residuals are listed above; the model does not reproduce guidance, mass flow or variable thrust. Powered descent and ascent remain two-anchor interpolations and do not represent guidance.
- Moon-fixed anchors use the IAU mean-Earth/polar-axis frame; the Apollo-era selenographic frame may differ by up to about a kilometre.
- UTC is used for time; UT1−UTC and TT−UTC (taken as 39.7 s for the lunar rotation) are approximations well under the 1 km tolerance.
- Launch, ascent to orbit, parachute descent and splashdown exist only as events.
