## Texture orientation

The equirectangular Three.js sphere puts texture-centre longitude 0 on mesh +X, longitude 90°E on -Z and the pole on +Y, which is the same fixed conversion as EQJ → render. Visual checks (India/Asia shape and terminator at 2026-04-01 12:00 UTC; near-side maria and Tycho at full Moon) show no extra yaw is needed, so `textureYawRad` in the Earth and Moon visual records (`src/assets/bodies/`) is 0.

**Iapetus proof (O-039 T-130, 2026-10-22).** The same mesh +X = map longitude 0 convention holds for Iapetus (code path: `applyOrientation` multiplies the IAU quaternion, conjugated by the EQJ → render axes, by the visual's yaw about +Y; mesh local -Z is 90°E, as `latLonToXYZ` places markers). Iapetus's map PIA18436 is registered 180° from the marker frame, so its visual uses `textureYawRad` = PI (`sphere()` in `planetSystemMaps.ts` takes the yaw, default 0). `tests/mapOrientation.test.ts` renders Iapetus at 2016-06-01 and shows the drawn dark centre (Cassini Regio, marker longitude 87.4°E) points 0.8° from Iapetus's velocity about Saturn with yaw PI and 179.2° from it with yaw 0 (tidally locked: the dark hemisphere leads). Gazetteer centres (USGS, planetographic +West): Cassini Regio 28.10°S 92.60°W (feature 1047), Engelier 40.50°S 264.70°W (feature 14463); both land on the columns the donor's marker frame (180 - W) expects, Engelier within 3° (stored -82, gazetteer gives -84.7). Owner's look on the preview decides whether other moons follow (I-057).

**Other moon turns and Titan's places (O-039 T-131, 2026-10-22).** After Iapetus, the audit's other 180 E-centred pictures turn half a revolution too: `textureYawRad` = PI for Europa, Callisto, Pluto, Tethys, Dione, Rhea and Titan; Ganymede goes from PI to 0 (the donor already recentred its mosaic by 180 degrees, `textureRecentringDeg`), so every other body is unchanged. `tests/mapOrientation.test.ts` pins each turn and checks two or three USGS Gazetteer longitudes per body against the map column the stored marker longitude names (Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov, looked up 2026-10-22: Pwyll 4878, Conamara Chaos, Valhalla 6284, Tros, Osiris, Sputnik Planitia 15669 (+East), Odysseus 4406, Penelope, Amata 215, Dido, Creusa, Tirawa 6026, Inktomi, Xanadu 6958, Ontario Lacus 7003). The Huygens landing site is not a Gazetteer name; its 10.573 S 192.335 W is from Wikipedia's Huygens (spacecraft) page.

Titan's `features.json` stays byte for byte as the donor wrote it: west longitudes used directly (its `map.json` says so: source positive-west, marker scale 1, offset 0), but the picture has 360 W at its left edge and 0 W at its right, so a west value W belongs at marker longitude 180 - W. `shownLongitude` in `src/experiences/planets/surfacePlace.mjs` applies that as the places load (`placedFeatures` in `planetDefinition.ts`), keyed by the map record's `coordinates` (positive-west source and an identity marker transform); the body packages now carry `map.json` for the Saturn system. No other record has that signature (Triton's identity transform is east-positive), so no other body changes. Xanadu stored 100 is drawn at 80, Ontario Lacus 183 at -3, Huygens 192.3 at -12.3.

**Europa's places (O-039 T-139, I-067, 2026-10-22).** Europa's `features.json` also stores USGS Gazetteer west longitudes (Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov, looked up 2026-10-22, planetographic +West: Tyre [6170] 146.6, Pwyll [4878] 271.4, Conamara Chaos [1282] 272.7, Murias Chaos [4074] 83.8, Thera Macula [5967] 181.2, Thrace Macula [5984] 172.1, Cyclades Macula [1361] 191.3, Castalia Macula [1048] 225.7, Cilix [1216] 181.9, Manannan [3622] 239.7, Tara Regio [14296] 75.0), so `data/bodies/europa/map.json` (regenerated by the importer from `tools/import/planetary-explorer-conventions.json`) now carries the same source-convention signature as Titan's (positive-west, marker scale 1, offset 0) and `shownLongitude` draws a west value W at 180 - W. `features.json` is untouched. Tyre stored 147 is drawn at 33 (it was 114 degrees off); Pwyll and Conamara Chaos near 270 W move by a couple of degrees only. Io (east-positive marker frame), Ganymede (scale -1) and Callisto (already 180 - W) were spot-checked against the Gazetteer and match as stored.

The asset licence record is `HYG_STAR_LICENCE` in `src/assets/sky.ts` (PROV-02). **CC BY-SA 4.0 applies to the star file only; the Astraeus code stays MIT.** Attribution line: "HYG database v4.2, astronexus (astronexus.com), CC BY-SA 4.0. Stars of V 6.5 or brighter kept, right ascension converted from hours to degrees; no other change." Against the Yale set, shared stars (paired by position within 30 arcsec, 98.8% pair) agree to a median 0.7 arcsec and 99% within 4 arcsec, with a median magnitude difference of 0.01; variable stars and split doubles differ more (`tests/hygStars.test.ts`).

The fetch tool copies only a file whose own provenance declares a licence on its shippable list (CC BY 4.0, CC0 1.0), so CAMS and the Yale catalogue, which declare none, cannot be shipped; the public-build validator keeps checking every other file under `data/local/` and still fails if any CAMS or Yale content appears in `dist`.

- The SVS page (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851, and its API https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/api/4851) states **no licence**. Its credit lines are "NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio." and "Credit: Gaia team/ESA", with "Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC" and the animator "Ernie Wright (USRA)". It adds: "While we identify the data sets used on this page, we do not store any further details, nor the data sets themselves on our site."
- The SVS help page (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/) says: "All of our content is in the public domain (unless otherwise noted)," and "meaning that it is free to download, use, and redistribute for whatever purposes you see fit." It links NASA's media guidelines and says nothing about third-party catalogue data.
- NASA's media usage guidelines (https://www.nasa.gov/multimedia/guidelines/index.html): "NASA occasionally uses copyright-protected material of third parties with permission on its website." and "NASA's use does not convey any rights to others to use the same material." "Those wishing to use copyright protected material of third parties must contact the copyright holder directly." "NASA should be acknowledged as the source of the material."
- The Gaia users' licence page (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia-users/license) says: "Gaia data are distributed under the CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO license." and sends commercial use to the ESA space science archives terms and conditions (that linked page was not read). The Gaia DR2 credit instruction is the acknowledgement "ESA/Gaia/DPAC". **That page names CC BY-NC, not CC BY-SA**: the share-alike licence (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO) appears on ESA's sky *images* such as the DR2 release picture, not on the catalogue data. A third-party registry tags DR2 as CC BY-SA, which was not relied on.
- Whether a NASA visualisation of Gaia (and Tycho-2 and Hipparcos-2) catalogue data inherits the Gaia non-commercial term, or is public domain as the SVS help says, is not stated anywhere read. The SVS page lists no "otherwise noted" restriction, but it also does not address the catalogue's own licence. This is unclear, so the cautious choice applies; no licence is assumed. If the owner later obtains a written answer from the SVS or ESA, the map can move to (a) with credit "NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio; Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC; visualisation by Ernie Wright (USRA)".

What the tool does: reads `starmap_2020_{4k,8k,16k,32k}.exr` (linear half-float, celestial equirectangular, RA 0h at the centre, RA increasing leftwards; 16k is 16384x8192, 422.9 MB, kept in `data/local/sky-src/`), applies `out = min(1, L x exposure)^(1/gamma)` (defaults exposure 10, gamma 2.2, chosen by eye on a 2048 px preview; ImageMagick 6 Q16 here is not HDRI, so values above 1.0 are clipped on read), and cuts tiles of at most 4096 px as JPEG (default quality 20, 4:2:0). At 16k that is 4x2 tiles of 4096x4096, 8.69 MB in all. `manifest.json` records the frame (`equatorial-j2000`), grid, source, credit, licence line, the tone settings and the default strength (`gain`, 1.0). Registration is exact: the map is already J2000 RA/Dec, so no rotation is applied, and Vega, Deneb, Sirius and Capella fall within one pixel of the predicted position in the 2048 px map and in the 16k tiles (`tests/skyPicture.test.ts`). The baked-in stars are the same stars as the catalogue's, so the bright ones sit under the drawn ones; at exposure 10 the map's own faint stars are weak beside them and the picture's strength stays at 1.0. `?skymap=eso` draws the ESO picture, and the app falls back to it when the tiles are absent. Before and after images: `docs/evidence/o011-i026-20261016T101356Z/` (`perseids-overview-*.png`, `sagittarius-patch-*.png`).
