The science

The methods under the pictures, and how each one was checked.

Time

UTC, TT, TDB, Julian dates

Every moment is converted once, in one place, so the Moon, the planets and the spacecraft all agree on what "now" means.

orb.js time scales

Tested

Frames

IAU 2006 precession · IAU 2000B nutation

Positions are held in one fixed sky frame (J2000) and converted for drawing. "Up" can be celestial north, or flat to the planets' plane.

IAU standards

Tested

Orbits

VSOP87A · two-body Kepler

Planets from a full published theory. Comets, asteroids and every meteor from classical orbital elements, solved per frame.

Precision at scale

Floating origin

The scene is re-centred on whatever you look at, so a body 51 AU out still sits still on screen. Measured: under a hundredth of a pixel of drift.

Reliable orbits

Peña-Asensio & Sánchez-Lozano 2024

A published quality cut: a wide enough viewing angle between cameras (15° or more) and a speed error of 10% or less. A switch between all and reliable.

Does the stream hit Earth on time?

Independent checks on the measured orbits · Global Meteor Network, 2025

The Perseid orbits pass closest to Earth 2.2 hours before the official peak. The radiant they imply is 0.04° from the IAU's, and the median speed of the members crossing near Earth is 58.75 km/s against the IAU's 58.8.

Rings of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune

Drawn, not pictures

Drawn in code as bands, with edges from NASA's planetary ring fact sheets (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet) and colours matched by eye to NASA spacecraft photos. Saturn, Uranus and Neptune match the fact sheets. Jupiter's bands sit about a fifth too close to the planet.

Gaps we know about.
