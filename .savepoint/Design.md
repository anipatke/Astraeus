# Astraeus — Design

## Architecture

The Earth–Moon spike has a small framework-independent scientific core and a separate rendering layer. Absolute UTC time feeds dated trajectories and body orientation; explicit center composition and physical Sun directions remain in EQJ kilometres; replaceable scale policies produce fresh render-scale values. A Float64 floating origin and fixed EQJ→render-axis conversion sit downstream, feeding a React Three Fiber scene, a generic viewer shell and app-owned developer diagnostics. Full scientific note: `docs/ASTRAEUS_SPIKE_01.md`; shell decisions: `docs/ASTRAEUS_SPIKE_03.md`.

Since O-014 a third layer sits between core and experiences: `src/assets/` holds the body catalogue (definition from `src/core/bodies/`, state-source binding, visual record, licence), named compositions and the texture files. A `StateResolver` composes any catalogued body to any catalogued centre through the parent chain; the scene mounts the bodies of a composition about a scene centre that is data; paths, scale policies and lighting are generic over the composition. Experiences (`src/experiences/`) are assembled from those parts. The name rule is enforced by `tests/nameCheck.test.ts` (see Interfaces, Name rule). Built-versus-planned record: `docs/ASTRAEUS_SPIKE_04_ASSET_ARCHITECTURE.md`, section "Built".

## Interfaces and Data Flow

`SimulationClock` produces integer UTC Unix milliseconds from a monotonic anchor. `MoonTrajectory.stateAt(time, center)` returns Moon State in EQJ for Earth or Sun centers. Earth orientation comes from the provider adapter; the Moon near-side axis and local pole basis come from the dated trajectory. `SampledTrajectory.stateAt(time)` returns Earth-centred EQJ km for a vehicle, cubic Hermite when velocity is present, and throws `TrajectoryOutOfRangeError` outside its `bounds`; the scene draws a tracked body only inside its bounds. `CenteredPosition` composition changes the origin while preserving body identity. `ScalePolicy` maps physical kilometres/radii without changing State. Sun geometry is represented by an explicit Sun/Earth position and derived physical directions/illuminated fraction. The public core has no Orb, React or Three.js types.

The app adapter builds `MissionConfig` from the generated Apollo samples and events. Each `TrackedBody` carries its trajectory, sample/anchor times, optional anchor labels, `positionDiscrepancies` and the trajectory's `Provenance`; `TrackedReadout.positionKm` is the current physical position alongside frame, centre, range and speed. `MissionConfig.journey` supplies the configured journey endpoints. `TrackedBodyView` draws the source anchors with a generated round sprite (`anchorSpriteTexture`) instead of untextured square points; the positions and count are unchanged.

`buildApollo11Experience` projects app/mission data into the generic shell contract. `ExperienceObject` carries configured `description`, `provenance`, `knownLimitations`, label priority and distance fields; the experience carries `cameraPresets`, events, time bounds, rates and `eventProvenance`. Generated trajectory provenance passes through `TrackedBody.provenance`; `src/app/ephemerisProvenance.ts` re-exports the Earth/Moon ephemeris records, which now live in `src/core/bodies/`. Metric values are calculated by app adapters from physical State and passed to the shell through `metricsFor`.

At startup `App` selects `true-scale`, and `SceneControls.policy` is `trueScale`. It passes `ExperienceConfig`, `ClockSnapshot`, `scaleId`, `metricsFor`, scene children and callbacks into `AstraeusShell`. The shell owns selection, active camera preset and open information-panel state. Selection and camera actions call back to app-owned refs; play, rate and seek callbacks operate the `SimulationClock`; the scale callback maps `true-scale`/`readable-scale` through `scalePolicyFor` to the existing `trueScale`/`readableScale` policies. Neither the shell nor a presentation change mutates scientific State. Developer diagnostics remain app-owned and are mounted only when the developer toggle is on.

### Experience plug-in boundary

`App` takes one `ExperienceDefinition` (`src/app/experienceDefinition.ts`) and never imports an experience. The definition holds the shell's `ExperienceConfig`, the clock start and rate, the `sceneLayers` it opts into (`tracked-bodies`, `points`, `sun-centred`; the `earth-moon` layer was retired in O-014, composition supplies the bodies), its `TrackedBody` list, a `createMetrics` factory and optional developer-panel sections. Tracked bodies are drawn only when that layer is listed. `src/main.tsx` is the only place that picks an experience. The scene mounts the bodies of the experience's composition; with no composition it mounts no bodies, lights or Moon path, makes no Moon or Sun calls, and frames the camera on the declared `sceneCenter` (the canvas carries `data-bodies`, empty then); `synthetic-bare` in the entry exercises this (I-015). Apollo (`src/experiences/apollo11/apollo11Definition.tsx`) and the synthetic fixture (`src/synthetic/`) both mount through it; neither `src/core/` nor `src/shell/` changed.

### Asset architecture (O-014)

**Catalogue and resolver.** A `CelestialBodyDefinition` (`src/core/body.ts`) holds a body's id, parent, radii, GM, orientation rule and `accuracy` (position record with `validUtc`); built-ins live in `src/core/bodies/` and carry their ephemeris records (the former `src/app/ephemerisProvenance.ts` is a re-export, ARCH-04). A `StateSourceBinding` gives each body its `Trajectory`. `createCatalogue(definitions, bindings, visuals)` takes three lists, so a visual can exist without a definition and be rejected. `StateResolver.stateOf(body, t, centre)` composes through the parent chain for any pair of catalogued ids; `sunFrom(centre, t)` returns the catalogue root as a `State`; `offsetKm` gives centre-to-centre offsets. Failures are `CatalogueError` (a `RangeError`). Composed States carry position only: no catalogued provider supplies velocity, and a provider that did would need velocity composition added to the resolver. `createState(input, requiresOrientation?)` takes the orientation rule from the resolver's definitions. `createOrbTwoBodyAdapter(definitions)` and `TwoBodyTrajectoryInput.center` carry a non-Sun GM to the two-body maths; `TwoBodyAdapter`, `OrbitGroupInput`, `State`, `Trajectory` and `Provenance` are unchanged. The resolver's memo holds 64 entries (FIFO).

**Visual records and one renderer.** `BodyVisual` records (`src/assets/visual.ts`: sphere or spheroid, texture reference with licence, atmosphere, material, light, marker, yaw) live per body in `src/assets/bodies/`. `BodyView` (`src/app/BodyView.tsx`, the React mount) draws from pure decisions in `src/app/bodyDraw.ts`: sphere with or without texture, atmosphere, marker (the Sun) and spheroid (Jupiter) with independently mapped equatorial and polar radii; `drawnRadii` and the focus limits pass the body id to `mapRadius`. `SunBody` is reduced to the Sun's shell item and validation; colour and marker size come from the catalogue Sun, and `visualOverrides` (`visible`, `exaggeration`, any appearance field merged over the record) is the experience's choice. A body with `visible: false` is not mounted but still resolves as a centre. `bodyMetrics.ts` and `catalogueItem.ts` sit in `src/app/`, not `src/assets/`, because they need shell types and `tests/layerRule.test.ts` lets `src/assets/` import only core, assets and three (ARCH-04).

**Compositions and scene centre.** `SystemComposition` (`src/assets/compositions.ts`) names catalogue ids, a default centre and declared `CompositionPath`s (`{body, spanMs, samples}`); it names no experience. `ExperienceDefinition.composition` is optional only for the bodiless fixture `synthetic-bare` (which then must declare `sceneCenter`); every shipped experience names one. `sceneCenterOf` defaults to the composition's centre and rejects a centre outside the composition. The scene (`AstraeusScene`, formerly `EarthMoonScene`) mounts one `BodyView` per composition body; `SceneRuntime` carries `resolver`, `bodies`, `composition`, `sceneCenter`; `ExperienceRuntime` is `{clock, resolver, catalogue}` (the separate `MoonTrajectory` and `adapter` are gone; the Apollo panels read the Moon through the resolver). The old `earth-moon` scene layer is retired; the canvas carries `data-scene-center`, `data-bodies` and `data-composition-paths`. Framing and layer assignment are in `src/app/sceneFraming.ts`: the centre body gets a minimum of 1.15 radii and a default maximum of max(3.2, 2.4 x span); other spheres 1.6 and 4 radii; markers are framed by their paths' reach. Camera reach is per experience (`ExperienceDefinition.camera`, O-011), which closes the camera-limits gap below. Moon-focus start side is generalised to "toward the body's parent".

**Path kinds.** `PathLayer` is `PathRule & PathSpec & {object}`; a rule is an orbit (a path about a parent, co-moving) or a trail (relative, inertial), and `span` is `TimeBounds` or a `RollingSpan {windowMs}` about the clock time (the Moon's monthly orbit). Rules live in `src/app/pathRules.ts`; `PathStyle` (opacity, depth test, layer, render order) is presentation and never reaches a rule. Path lines draw after data dots (render order 1 against 0), so a path stays visible across a belt (I-020). The resolver supplies the root frame through `sunFrom`, so no literal "sun" is needed in generic code. Earth's year in Perseids is declared by the experience (fixed one-year span about the peak, 361 samples); the parent comet's orbit is drawn for peak plus or minus 180 days only (the full 109P orbit reaches about 51 AU, beyond the declared camera reach) and is `illustrative`, because its place along the orbit is set by the stream's node choice. The planets' orbit paths come from the catalogue (every body whose parent is the root and not in the composition), so a catalogued planet adds its orbit to every Sun-centred shower.

**Scale policies.** `ScaleId` is a plain string. `SolarSystemScalePolicy` keeps distances true and multiplies radii by a declared per-body factor (`factorFor(bodyId?)`, `explanation`); `scalePolicyFor(id, supplied?)` accepts application-supplied policies and `AstraeusShell` takes an optional `scaleChoices` prop that passes to `ScaleControl`. `App` builds the solar-system policy from the experience's `exaggeration` overrides (default factor 1). Jupiter-Io offers True scale only: Readable (distances x0.1) would bury Io inside Jupiter (orbit 5.9 Jupiter radii drawn at 0.59), and the solar-system scale enlarges both bodies.

**Planets.** `AstronomyAdapter` gained `planetPositionFromSunKm(planet, t)` and `daysSinceJ2000Tt(t)` (the IAU models count TT days; only the adapter imports Orb). The adapter registers the full VSOP87A series (the compact default is off by 2,877 to 142,044 km), costing about 1.4 MB raw (0.56 MB gzip) of bundle: at O-014 the main bundle went from 1,537.6 kB (458.8 kB gzip) to 2,938.7 kB (1,022.5 kB); 2,947.5 kB (1,025.1 kB) at T-050. I-022 (`1eb8b05`) moved the series out: `src/core/planetSeries.ts` exposes `loadPlanetSeries()` (one cached dynamic import of `planetModels.ts`), `planetModels()` (throws before loading rather than falling back to the compact tables) and `isPlanetId`; `src/app/planetNeeds.ts` (`needsPlanetSeries`) tells `src/main.tsx` whether the chosen definition mounts or is centred on a planet, and only then does `start()` await the load before rendering. Each experience is itself a dynamic import in `main.tsx`. Main chunk 3,041.8 to 1,185.1 kB (`docs/evidence/i022-20261016T115528Z/`); Apollo and Earth-Moon never download the planet chunk. Rotation data sit beside each definition (`MARS_ROTATION` etc., with a citation) and `planetTrajectory.ts` is the one generic planet source. Position maxima against JPL Horizons over six dates (1969-07-21 to 2030-06-01, which is each planet's `validUtc`): Mercury 30.4 km, Venus 50.1 km, Mars 95.8 km, Jupiter 1,269.4 km (0.325 arcsec). Planets are in the catalogue (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter); none is mounted by Apollo or Earth-Moon.

**Jupiter and Io.** `jupiterIoSystem` mounts about Jupiter, a centre that is neither Earth nor the Sun, through the same binding, resolver, composition, scene and shell as the Moon, with no change to `StateSourceBinding`, `CelestialBodyDefinition`, `createTwoBodyTrajectory`, `ScalePolicy`, `SystemComposition` or `ExperienceDefinition`. Io is published JPL mean elements about Jupiter's GM through the two-body provider, wrapped with tidal-lock orientation inside `core/bodies/io.ts` (a generic tidally-locked wrapper is a candidate to lift when a third moon arrives). Measured against Horizons at six dates the maximum Jupiter-centred error is 9,387.47 km, 2.58 Io diameters (3,642.98 km): outside the one-diameter line, so Io is an `approximate demonstration` (`reconstructed`, figure and the source's own warning in `knownLimitations`); at J2000 it is 825.5 km. The retrograde apsidal-motion sign is not stated by the source and was chosen to reproduce Io's period. Jupiter carries a Solar System Scope 2k map, CC BY 4.0, based on NASA imagery (I-024, `src/assets/bodies/jupiter.ts`, page opened 2026-10-16); its yaw is a visual stand-in because the bands rotate differentially, and the donor colour is the fallback under it. Io uses the donor's USGS public-domain map.

**Lighting choice (Decision 4).** Each lit body keeps its own directional light along its own physical Sun direction, on its own render layer, in a pass loop over the composition (pure `bodyLightDirections` in `src/app/lighting.ts`; the cap is the render layers, 29 to 31 lit bodies depending on the Task note, not measured). A single point light at the Sun's scaled position was measured against it (`?lighting=single-sun`, a measurement mode): illuminated fraction differs by at most 7.2e-9 and direction by 1.5e-6 degrees on the four USNO phase events at True and Readable scales; rendered Earth, Jupiter and Io frames differ by at most 0.006 of 255 on average; Moon frames differ by 7.7 to 13.0 of 255 because one light has one strength (Earth is tuned 1.8/0.18, the Moon 1.4/0.08), and by 0.0005 to 0.009 once given the Moon's strength. A single directional light at the centre's direction would be 0.16 degrees off at the Moon. Decision: keep per-body lights for Spike 04; the single point light is viable for larger systems, at the cost of moving per-body intensity into material or per-body exposure. `illumination.ts` computes `illuminatedFraction` by role (any satellite, the Sun as source); `lunarIlluminatedFraction` is a wrapper with its Moon guard.

**Texture rights.** Every texture is data on its visual record (`licence.ts`): either a recorded licence or `localOnly`, never both. Since I-029 (`a2f82e0`) no texture is `localOnly`: Earth is NASA Blue Marble Next Generation (July 2004, public domain) and the Moon is the NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit LROC WAC map (SVS 4720, public domain); Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter are Solar System Scope CC BY 4.0 (the Jupiter page was opened 2026-10-16; Mercury, Venus and Mars rest on the donor README); Io is USGS public domain per the donor README. The mechanism stays: a public build (`ASTRAEUS_PUBLIC_BUILD=1`) replaces each module named in the local-only list in `vite.config.ts` by `src/assets/textures/public-placeholder.png` at the import (the list is now empty), and `tools/validate/public-build-assets.mjs` hashes `dist/` against the local-only sources, `data/local/` and the Yale file. The donor's remaining planet and moon pictures are traced but not shipped (I-032, open).

**Name rule.** `tests/nameCheck.test.ts` forbids experience names everywhere in `src/core/`, `src/assets/`, `src/shell/` and generic `src/app/`, and body names (`"sun"`, `"earth"`, `"moon"`, `EARTH`, `MOON`, `SUN`) everywhere except `src/core/bodies/` and `src/assets/bodies/` and ten allowlisted files, each with a stated reason: `src/assets/compositions.ts`, `src/core/{astronomyAdapter,body,illumination,moonTrajectory,referenceCenters,scalePolicy,state,sunCentredSources,twoBodyAdapter}.ts`. This differs from design section E check 7 ("`src/assets/` may name bodies; no body name in `src/core/`"): the core providers are the Orb boundary and the root of the body chain, so the allowlist stands, and a stale entry fails the test. Section E check 7 is amended to say so; moving the providers under `src/core/bodies/` is not planned.

**Precision at 1 AU (T-045).** A true-scale Earth 1 AU from the Sun marker, projected in Float64 from the Float32 buffers the GPU receives for 60 frames (`browser-precision.mjs`, 1280x800, True scale, headless software renderer): static displacement 1e-9 px (Earth) and 3e-8 px (path vertex); camera-sweep residual 0.00058 px (Earth) and 0.00056 px (path), 0.0013 px zoomed out. The Float32 grid at 1 AU is 12.4 km (5.4e-6 px at the Sun view). The focused body never exceeds 1 px (floating origin, threshold unchanged). The figures exclude the GPU's own Float32 matrix arithmetic. The T-050 re-run reproduces them (sun-focus sweep 0.00058 px Earth, 0.00056 px path).

**Moon `validUtc`.** The Moon's `accuracy.validUtc` is the sampled range, 2022-06-01 to 2025-06-01, not a model validity; Apollo (1969) lies outside it. Planets and Io use their sampled ranges likewise (planets 1969-07-21 to 2030-06-01); Earth 1900 to 2100. Only the shower builder reads `validUtc` today (to state where Jupiter's orbit is extrapolated past 2030). The Earth-Moon Explorer's window is 2025-01-01 to 2025-04-01, inside the Moon's checked range, not the design sketch's 2026.

#### Groups and the `points` layer (O-009)

A group is many objects with one scientific State source. `PointGroup` (`src/core/pointGroup.ts`) declares count, centre (Earth or Sun are composed), EQJ frame, optional time bounds and provenance, fills every member's EQJ km position into a caller-owned buffer for a UTC time, and returns one member as an ordinary `State` (body id `<group id>:<index>`). `createOrbitGroup` is the generic orbit-based group: base elements in a named plane (`ecliptic-j2000` or `EQJ`), a uniform half-width spread per element, a seed (same seed and time give bit-identical positions), two-body propagation through `TwoBodyAdapter`, and non-bound members rejected. `State`, `Trajectory`, `Provenance`, `TimelineEvent` and `ScalePolicy` are unchanged.

An experience opts in with the `points` layer and `groups: GroupLayer[]` on its definition; `groupsOf` and `experienceWithGroups` give one deduplicated shell item per group, carrying the group's provenance, bounds and limitations. `PointsView` (`src/app/PointsView.ts`) holds two exact fills per group, one at or before the clock time and one at or after it, `refillAfterMs` apart (default 5 minutes), and draws a straight-line blend of them each frame so dots glide; playing on slides the pair and fills one new end per span, a seek fills once (presentation only, State untouched; at a fill time the drawn positions are exact; the blend is off by about 8 km at worst on a 26,000 km ring at the default span, measured in T-030). It composes Sun-centred members to the scene's Earth-centred placement through existing centre composition, then applies `ScalePolicy`, render axes and the floating origin. A group is hidden outside its bounds with no fill. Its focus, follow and label point is the centroid; its id is a `FocusId`. Camera presets for a group come from the experience.

**Frame-rate counter (T-031).** `FrameStats` (`src/app/frameStats.ts`) is a pure fixed-window (120 frames, no allocation per frame) collector of frame durations; it reports frames per second over the last second, the longest recent frame and frames over 33.4 ms, and restarts after a gap over 1 s (a paused tab). `App` creates it only while Developer mode is on and hands it to the scene through `SceneControls.frameStats` (null means nothing is measured); the scene ticks it once per frame and copies each `PointsView.fillCount` into `SceneControls.groupFills`. The existing 250 ms poll reads both into `FrameRateSection` (`src/app/FrameRateSection.tsx`), a Developer tools section that carries a note that the figures are for this browser and machine. Presentation only: no State, scale or camera involvement, no new metric kind, no `src/core/` or `src/shell/` change.

#### Time windows and rates (O-010)

A window is a labelled period that lasts, independent of any trajectory. `TimelineWindow` (`src/core/timeWindow.ts`) has an id, label, start, end, an optional peak inside it, optional rate samples with a unit label, and optional provenance spans inside it. `createWindows` validates and freezes them with named errors (start before end, peak and samples inside, sorted unique sample times, finite non-negative values, sorted non-overlapping spans, unique ids). `rateAt` is linear between samples and null outside the window, outside the samples or without a rate. `provenanceAt` returns the span covering a time (a shared boundary belongs to the later span) or null; a window without spans falls back to the experience's `eventProvenance`. `TimelineEvent`, `State`, `Trajectory`, `Provenance` and `ScalePolicy` are unchanged, and nothing in a window is derived from a State (ARCH-02).

`ExperienceConfig.windows` is optional. An experience without it (Apollo) renders and navigates exactly as before. The shell draws each window on the timeline bar as a shaded band, a faint rate curve and a peak marker (`timelineModel` builds positions and the curve path). The context row adds an "Inside" line with the current rate while the clock is in a window. `navigationStops` merges event times with each window's start, peak and end for Previous/Next; where an event and a window point share a time the event is the stop. Opening a window uses a `window` info target and `infoView(..., timeUtcMs)`, so its rate and source depend on the clock and the source wording changes across a span boundary. A new shell metric kind `rate` ("N per unit time") reuses value, unit, range and context and renders through the existing Metric Visuals; an experience supplies it through `createMetrics` from the same window samples (`rateAt`), so the curve and the readout share one source.

Made-up evidence (T-035): `src/synthetic/syntheticDefinition.ts` declares a "Contact window" with a rising and falling "passes per hour" rate, a peak, and two `illustrative` provenance spans whose accuracy and notes differ. Its object's metrics add a contact-rate readout (unavailable outside the window). No generic code was needed to mount it. One generic fix was needed: the window layer sat below the seek slider, so the band and peak could not be clicked; its stacking was raised to match the event markers.
